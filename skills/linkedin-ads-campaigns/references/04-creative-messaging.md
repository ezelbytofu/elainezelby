# 04 - Creative and messaging

Creative is the biggest lever on performance. Nailing the message matters more than the
targeting or the account structure. Offer + messaging beat the ads themselves.

## The style rules (they feel backwards until you see the results)

- **Ads that look like organic beat ads that look like corporate ads.** Most LinkedIn ads
  look like LinkedIn ads, so looking even slightly different wins.
- **Avoid corporate blue.** The whole platform is blue; stand out by not being blue.
- **Scrappy beats polished, often ~3x.** A selfie shot on a phone outperforms a beautiful
  $50k brand video where the first 5 seconds fade from black into a logo. At bottom of
  funnel, get playful: memes, humor, a bit rough around the edges.
- **Square (1080x1080)** takes more feed space than landscape, so more attention. (Neil
  Patel's guide cites 1200x627 for single-image ads; the agency operators prefer 1:1 for
  feed real estate. Test both; default to square for reach.)
- **Grade-5 reading level.** No word salad. If a child cannot understand the ad, an adult
  will not spend brain power decoding it.
- **~5 ad variations per ad set** to find winners.
- Video does **not** need professional production. A scrappy talking-to-camera clip with
  helpful tips beats the studio brand film.

## Context: message changes by temperature

- **Cold / top of funnel: lead with PAIN, in their exact words.** Do not talk about
  yourself ("we're award-winning" = nobody cares). The win is when they read it and think
  "how does this person know exactly what I'm dealing with?" Pain outperforms desire on
  cold audiences.
- **Warm / bottom of funnel: offer + PROOF.** Testimonials, result screenshots, case
  studies, how-it-works. Show you can deliver what you promise.

## Thought-leadership ads (the workhorse)

Promote a post from a **personal profile** as a paid ad. It appears in the feed looking
like an organic post ("Promoted"), not a company ad. Setup:
1. Post from a personal profile about a pain the audience feels.
2. In Campaign Manager, new campaign, objective = Engagement (cheapest reach).
3. Target the exact people you will later DM.
4. In the ads step, "browse existing content" and paste the post URL.
5. Let organic signal guide you: boost the posts that already got the most organic
   engagement.

### Executive thought leadership pays (LinkedIn's own data)
- Founders who post **>=10x/year** (roughly monthly) generate **33% more leads** than
  founders who do not.
- When a buying-committee member **engages** with an exec's post, deal size lifts ~24%;
  when they **follow** the exec, deal size is up to **120%** larger. Personal engagement
  breaks trust barriers and speeds the cycle.
- **Ads + exec content compound:** audiences warmed by thought-leadership ads before a
  sponsored message convert at **6x** vs the sponsored message alone. Tying exec content to
  paid drives a 20-30% lift, including on other channels (people go search for you).
- Content mix: **80% true thought leadership** (audience pain, industry trends, leadership
  lessons, "what I learned building the product"), **20%** solution/product. Inform and
  teach, do not pitch. "What I learned building X" is a veiled, effective way to talk about
  the product.
- Treat a post as a **conversation, not a broadcast:** ask questions, reply to comments,
  test future posts in the comment section.
- Authenticity over polish. Any format the exec is comfortable with (selfie video walking,
  or plain text) works. To unblock a busy exec: think like a journalist about your day
  (trends from customer calls, biggest industry challenges, why you evolved the product).
- **AI caveat:** use AI to organize/summarize (upload meeting notes, cut a draft), never to
  create the thought itself. AI regurgitates consensus; it cannot produce thought leadership
  that does not exist yet, and audiences can tell.
- **Creators/customers:** customer and third-party creator posts often outperform your own
  employees'. Six people saying you are good beats you saying it. Promoting someone's
  thought leadership via ads is symbiotic; they rarely ask for payment. Thought-leader ads
  can put someone with **no following** in front of exactly the right people.

## Content types and ratio (organic + boosted)

- **Grow** - stories, personal experience, observations. Builds reach and likeability.
- **Educate** - teach how to solve the problem you fix. Lower reach, nurtures followers,
  positions you as expert.
- **Capture** - direct CTA ("DM me X and I'll send the framework," "comment for the
  template"). Conversion posts.
- **Ratio: 80% give (grow + educate), 20% ask (capture).**

## The 5-step post structure

1. **Hook** - a bold claim or contradiction in the first line (all people see before
   deciding to keep reading).
2. **Stakes** - make them feel the cost of the problem, in **their** words ("drowning in
   manual work," not "experiencing operational inefficiencies").
3. **What changed** - the turning point; what you or a client did differently. Be specific;
   numbers help.
4. **Lesson** - pull out the principle the reader can apply. Positions you as expert without
   claiming to be one.
5. **CTA** - one easy next step framed as helping, not selling ("DM me to get X", or a
   landing-page link).

## Ad copy specifics (Neil Patel / LinkedIn guidance)

- Sponsored content: headline <150 chars, description <=70 chars, up to 5 ads per campaign.
- CTAs: action-oriented and specific ("download the guide", "register") beat vague; even
  "Learn more" converts if the copy built enough value first.
- One color palette/style across your ads for recognition. High-quality, attention-grabbing
  visuals; humor lifts click-through.

## Verticalized social proof (a distinct, powerful play)

When you have a visual differentiator or strong case studies, verticalize them by industry
rather than running generic testimonials:
- **Show, don't tell.** Show the product in use and the end result, not just a quote about
  results. Underused and high-impact for good products.
- **B2B FOMO is "fear of messing up," not fear of missing out.** Featuring big-brand logos
  (e.g. "we did this for Walmart/L'Oreal") de-risks the decision for smaller buyers: "if it
  worked for them and something goes wrong, I won't be blamed."
- **Fresh-start effect:** people in new roles (first ~6 months) are more willing to act
  ambitiously, so recent-job-changers are a strong behavioral target for these ads.
- Verticalize case studies so the ad says "this is a situation where you should think of
  us." Builds **mental availability** so you are the brand recalled when the buyer is ready.
- Long-form video (e.g. 4+ min) earns mental availability from deep viewers; then refresh
  those people with static ads over time. Track consumption/dwell to find which resonate and
  make more like the winners.
