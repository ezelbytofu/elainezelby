# 06 - Outreach and the conversion system

Ads generate attention and warm audiences. This layer converts them. In B2B most deals
close over weeks or months across many touchpoints, so the follow-up system matters as much
as the lead gen.

## Your profile is your landing page

Every ad, comment, and DM sends people to your profile. Fix it before driving traffic:
- **Photo:** run headshots through **Photofeeler** (free); pick one scoring >8 on
  competence, likeability, and influence. It appears on every message, comment, and
  thought-leader ad.
- **Headline:** who you help + the result, not your job title. "Sales Director at X" triggers
  sales resistance instantly. Match the seniority of who you target (founder-to-founder beats
  SDR-to-CEO).
- **Banner:** state the outcome in plain terms. Formula: "We help [audience] achieve
  [tangible outcome] in [time period]." e.g. "We help HR reps cut staff turnover 21% in 60
  days."
- **Featured section:** two links, one to a lead magnet, one to book a call. This turns the
  profile into a native landing page that can generate a lead with no action from you.
- **About:** speak to the client's pain, not a CV.
- **Pronouns field hack:** it shows in message threads, so use it for a short context line
  about what you do (a free touchpoint most people ignore). Only do this if the field is not
  needed for its actual purpose.

## Build the network (the Dream 100)

- Use **Sales Navigator** (core tier is enough). Filter by industry, headcount, geography,
  role, then add **behavioral filters**: e.g. companies growing >20% (they need talent),
  or people who recently changed roles (new hires make the most changes in their first 6
  months to prove themselves).
- Sales Navigator accuracy is imperfect: rip and clean the list with **Evaboot**, then
  enrich with **Clay** (its AI can check things LinkedIn cannot, like whether they use a
  specific tool, in plain English). Narrowing to genuine fits lifts CTR, cuts CPL, and
  improves lead quality (one operator doubled CTR and halved cost-per-close just by changing
  *who* saw the ads, not the ads themselves).
- **Connect without a note.** ~66% acceptance without a note vs ~34% with one. A blank
  request lets your (now-optimized) photo and headline do the work. Automate connection
  **requests** (Dripify, LinkedIn Helper) but **never automate the messages**.
- **Volume hacks to message without connecting / without InMail:** join LinkedIn **Groups**
  in your niche and message members directly; sign up for LinkedIn **Events** and use the
  networking tab.

## The human-touch arbitrage

Everyone is automating DMs, so AI messages all read the same and the trust threshold has
risen. The winning move is to zag: be genuinely human. This is the single biggest arbitrage
on LinkedIn right now.
- **Stop using AI for DMs and comments.** Do them for real.
- **Specificity is the test:** if a message could go to 1,000 people unchanged, it is not
  good enough. Reference something real (a post they wrote, their company, the lead magnet
  they downloaded).
- **Loom videos:** pull up their LinkedIn profile in the background so they see themselves
  on screen; say their name; reference their situation or a relevant case study. Response
  rates jump (one operator went from ~800 to ~200 cost-per-call in a day). ~1 booked call
  per ~20 videos is a realistic ratio.
- **Voice notes** (in LinkedIn mobile DMs) get the same uplift, faster to produce. Proves a
  human, stands out against walls of text.

## The Qualifying Questions Method (5 steps)

The most reliable outreach structure. It should feel like a conversation, never a pitch.
1. **Open (give before you take).** Warm lead: "Hey [name], just checking you got access to
   [the lead magnet]?" (the highest-performing first message tested, because it helps rather
   than pitches). Cold lead: a specific, genuine reference to their post/profile.
2. **Ask about their situation.** "Out of curiosity, are you currently dealing with [the
   problem you solve]?" You are qualifying and helping them name the problem to themselves.
3. **Dig.** How long has it been an issue? What have they tried? The more they articulate the
   pain, the more they realize they need help.
4. **Offer help.** Literally: "Do you want some help with that?" Not "want to book a call."
5. **Share the call, framed around their problem.** "I'll look at your situation and give you
   a plan to fix [their specific problem]. Zero pressure." The call is about them, not your
   pitch.

**Convert LinkedIn leads on LinkedIn.** DMs beat email and phone for LinkedIn-sourced leads
(they are already on the platform, in that headspace). Lead with LinkedIn, then email/call.

## Sponsored messaging: use MESSAGE ADS, not conversation ads

- Same-content tests across many accounts: **message ads ~55% open rate vs conversation ads
  ~35%**. At equal CTR that means conversation ads cost roughly **2x per conversion**.
- Conversation ads' only edge (retargetable opens/clicks, multiple CTAs) does not matter,
  because sponsored messaging runs at **bottom of funnel** where you are not building
  retargeting audiences anyway. Also, the forced "Not interested" CTA on conversation ads
  suppresses future delivery.
- Rule: keep messages short and simple so they qualify as a **message ad**, run them to
  bottom of funnel, and accept losing retargetability.
- **Sponsored-messaging frequency caps** (to plan sender/creative rotation): a dynamic
  **7-18 day** cap per member (by activity level), a **30-day** cap per **sender**, and a
  **30-day** cap per **creative ID**. Using multiple senders (brand, CEO, head of product)
  and multiple creatives lets you reach the same person more often.

## Lead follow-up sequence

The moment a lead comes in:
1. Connect on LinkedIn and send the step-1 opener ("just checking your access to [lead
   magnet]"). Human, no agenda. This separates you from everyone who sent a sales email or
   did nothing.
2. Work the qualifying questions on reply.
3. Once a call is booked, prime the show: automated confirmation + reminders at 24h and 3h,
   each linking a short video on how the service works and the outcomes. Send a LinkedIn
   message a few hours before ("looking forward to it, ping me if any trouble joining"). By
   call time it is logistics, not persuasion.

## After the call (long cycles)

- Add the prospect's **company and other stakeholders** to remarketing audiences so every
  decision-maker keeps seeing you.
- Send a short **Loom recap** they can share internally with other stakeholders.
- Connect on LinkedIn so your organic content enters their feed; send case studies and
  useful resources over the following weeks, framed as helpful regardless of whether they
  buy.
- Keep stacking touchpoints until the timing is right. The best system is the one still
  visible and relevant when the buyer is finally ready to move.
