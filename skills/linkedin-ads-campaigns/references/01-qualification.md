# 01 - Qualification: should you run LinkedIn Ads at all?

LinkedIn ads are a **scaling mechanism for a proven offer sold to a known ICP**.
They do not create product-market fit. Practitioners who tried to make LinkedIn
work for "anyone" found it failed no matter how good the campaigns were, while
sloppy accounts with the right offer pulled in clients. Run this gate before
spending anything.

## The four qualification questions

Answer all four. A "no" on any one means LinkedIn will feel broken, and it will
not be LinkedIn's fault.

1. **Economics: is the price point high enough?**
   - Service should be priced at a **minimum ~$5,000**, ideally $10,000+ lifetime value.
   - Average cost of acquisition on LinkedIn is **~$3,000 +/- $1,500**.
   - If average order value is ~$500, the math never works. You lose money per client.
   - The higher the price point, the easier LinkedIn gets.
   - Nuance (AJ Wilcox): the old rule of thumb was LTV > ~$10k. Cheap retargeting
     funnels (see CPRM in `05`) now make lower LTVs viable because cost-per-touch
     has collapsed. Still, be honest about unit economics.

2. **Cold offer: can you already acquire clients beyond network and referrals?**
   - If the only buyers so far are people who already knew you, you have a **warm
     offer, not a cold offer**. Pushing a warm offer to a cold audience burns budget.
   - Proof of a cold offer = you already convert cold traffic via content, cold
     email, or another paid channel. Then LinkedIn is just more fuel in a running engine.

3. **Known ICP: do you know your buyer from real sales data, not theory?**
   - "We think we'd be a good fit for X" is a theory. LinkedIn is too expensive to
     test theories.
   - You need proven data: exact roles, who has buying power, what drove the decision.
   - Best method (see `03-campaign-setup.md`): pull the LinkedIn profiles of
     **closed-won** clients, extract title / industry / company size / geography as
     shown on LinkedIn, and derive the ICP from real buyers.

4. **Presence: does your audience actually use LinkedIn?**
   - B2B professionals, founders, directors, department heads, senior hires: yes.
   - E-commerce founders, most consumer buyers: they have profiles but live on
     Instagram/TikTok/X. Advertise where they actually spend time.
   - By simply being an active LinkedIn user, a buyer pre-qualifies as a professional,
     which is why LinkedIn leads cost more up front but convert warmer and close bigger
     (one practitioner found LinkedIn CPL was 3x Facebook's, but the cost to land a
     paying client was **lower** because the leads were serious, not window-shoppers).

## Budget floor

- **Minimum ~$3,000-$5,000 per month** to gather enough data to find winners and cut
  losers. Below this you reach month-end without enough signal to know what worked.
- Minimum **~$50/day per engagement campaign** or it will not get enough distribution
  or bid competitiveness to generate meaningful data.
- If $5k/month feels like too much, LinkedIn is probably not the right platform right
  now, which loops back to question 1 (price point).

## If they fail the gate

Do not just say "run ads anyway." Name the failing gate and steer:
- Low price point → fix packaging/offer or use a cheaper channel.
- Warm-only offer → prove a cold offer on content or cold email first.
- Fuzzy ICP → do the closed-won profile analysis before spending.
- Audience not on LinkedIn → advertise where they are.
