# 02 - Funnel strategy

The winning structure is a staged funnel that warms cold audiences before ever
asking for a call. Two mental models power it:

- **The 7-hour / multiple-hits rule.** People need roughly 7 hours of exposure,
  across multiple contexts, before they trust you enough to buy. Seeing you Monday,
  Wednesday, Friday in different formats stacks as familiarity, and familiarity is
  the currency you trade for clients. Most advertisers ask for the sale at minute one.
- **Messaging shifts by stage.** Top of funnel = their **pain**, in their exact words.
  Bottom of funnel = your **offer + proof**.

## The pyramid reframe (invest more per person as they progress)

For B2B, the "funnel" is really a **pyramid**: the further a prospect moves, the **more**
content and dollars you put in front of each remaining person, not less. Reason: **timing is
the hardest thing in B2B.** Someone can be a perfect fit but not ready (budget, approvals,
procurement can take months). So the more interest they show, the more you invest to nurture
and stay present until the timing is right.

Split the pyramid into two halves:
- **Demand generation (top half):** get the message out to ICP prospects, share your POVs,
  benefits, and pain points, and let them self-select by engaging. Goal = reach + opt-in.
- **Demand capture (bottom half):** retarget the people who showed real intent and present
  the offer (demo / trial / call).

### Segment by persona first
If you have multiple personas (e.g. IT vs finance leaders), **segment them and give each its
own pyramid and content.** Ads read very differently per persona. In enterprise accounts,
match the messenger to the target: boost finance thought leaders' posts to finance buyers,
marketing to marketing, sales to sales.

### Intent-gate the retargeting (the biggest retargeting mistake)
**Do not enroll someone in bottom-of-funnel demo/trial ads just because they clicked or
watched top-of-funnel content. People click on anything.** A like or a video view is not buying
intent. Require a real intent signal, the simplest being a visit to your **website or a
high-intent page** (or company page), before showing the main offer. Skipping this gives you a
big but **watered-down** retargeting pool full of people who like your content but are not
decision-makers or not ready. So: do lots of on-platform retargeting at the top, but wait for
a site / high-intent visit before enrolling in demand-capture campaigns. This concentrates
spend on the highest-intent people and maximizes ROI.

Data point (Impactable): retargeting **demo-page visitors** (30-90 day window) with a
**document ad + lead-gen form** combo produces a **2.5x higher demo-to-close rate** than
standard retargeting.

### Match funnel length to the sales cycle
**Do not make the funnel longer than it needs to be.** Active demand + a ~1-month sales cycle?
Skip brand awareness and lead magnets and go straight to the requested demo / free trial. Long
enterprise cycles (6-18 months+) genuinely need the full pyramid: awareness → leads → engage /
opportunity → nurture.

### Opportunity stage: bring everything
Once an account becomes an opportunity, load its CRM list into a bottom-funnel campaign (ABM)
and bring **all** your marketing content to get the deal over the line. Mine sales-call
transcripts and talk to sales to find the exact questions and objections in the buying process,
then make content that answers each: case studies from similar-industry companies, social proof
(awards, testimonials from lookalike companies), real urgency/scarcity (never faked),
founder/exec thought leadership (people buy from people, not logos), and ungated tutorials/demo
videos so they can picture themselves getting the result.

## The three stages

### Stage 1 - Top of funnel (awareness / engagement)
- **Objective:** Engagement. Cheapest way to get in front of people.
- **Format:** Thought-leadership ads (boost a post from a **personal profile**, not the
  company page). People engage with people, not logos.
- **Content:** Talk about the pain the audience feels; educate; give one insight. Include
  a soft CTA (link to a landing page, or "DM me").
- **Purpose:** Not conversions. Familiarity, plus it builds your retargeting audiences.
  Anyone who likes/comments/clicks has raised their hand.

### Stage 2 - Middle of funnel (warm remarketing / lead capture)
- **Audience:** Everyone who engaged with Stage 1 content, plus website visitors and
  company-page visitors. A **60-day** window works well in most cases (can widen to 180
  for more volume).
- **Objective:** Lead generation, using LinkedIn's **native lead-gen forms**.
- **Offer:** A lead magnet directly tied to your offer. Formula: put "how to" in front of
  your offer. If you deliver "10 qualified sales calls a month," the lead magnet is
  "how to get 10 qualified sales calls a month."
- **Why native forms:** lower friction (no leaving LinkedIn) and they capture the
  prospect's **LinkedIn profile URL**, which is gold because LinkedIn-sourced leads
  convert best in LinkedIn DMs (see `06`).
- Note: lead-gen forms now support **dynamic UTM parameters** (ad set ID/name), so one
  form can be reused across ad sets and still track which ad set produced the lead.

### Stage 3 - Bottom of funnel (conversion)
- **Audience:** Only people who moved through the middle (warm).
- **Objective:** Website visits (push to a landing/booking page), plus message ads.
- **Formats to run in parallel:**
  - **Single image ads** (website visits) → landing/booking page.
  - **Video** testimonials / case studies / how-it-works (builds familiarity fastest).
  - **Message ads** (LinkedIn's native DM ad) → the scalable way to DM prospects.
    Use **message ads, not conversation ads** (see `06` for the open-rate data).
  - **Text ads** (very cheap) → keep your face/logo/name in front of people for
    familiarity. This is the cheapest way to stack the 7 hours on autopilot.
- **Messaging:** now you can talk about your offer, but **proof** matters most:
  testimonials, result screenshots, simple guides showing how the service works.

### Post-call remarketing (the layer most people skip)
After a call that has not closed, manually add the prospect's company and its other
stakeholders to your remarketing audiences. Every decision-maker there keeps seeing your
testimonials, results, and content. You become unavoidable inside the account, which lifts
close rate on long B2B cycles. In B2B, timing is often the variable: the best system is
the one still visible and relevant when the buyer is finally ready to move.

## The CPRM lens (how to pick what runs at each stage)

The real optimization question is: **what is the cheapest way to graduate someone to the
next stage?** That is **CPRM = Cost Per Retargetable Member** = spend / number of people
who took the retargetable action. Every format and objective yields a different CPRM.
Ranked best-to-cheapest by the practitioner who coined it:

1. **Video thought-leader ads** - best cost + quality. Cheap (video), and a retargetable
   view means they watched 25-50% (roughly 15-20 sec of a 30-40 sec video). With animated
   subtitles, CPRM lands at **$0.50-$1**. Paying more means bidding too high, using max
   delivery, or the video is not holding attention.
2. **Static thought-leader ads** - run in the Engagement objective, ~$1 per engagement =
   ~$1 CPRM. But an "engagement" can be shallow (clicking "...more", a like, a profile
   click), so the interaction quality is lower than video.
3. **Standard video ads (from company page)** - good quality. **Lowest CPRM comes from
   running video on the Website Visits objective**: lots of people watch, few click, and
   you only pay on the click, so you build cheap retargeting pools. (Video Views objective
   charges per 2-sec view and is only cheap if completion is high; Engagement is in between.)
4. **Document ads on the Website Visits objective (the hack)** - the **cheapest CPRM of
   all, ~$0.02-$0.03/person**. Documents are highly engaging (>10% flip to the next slide),
   and each engager becomes retargetable while almost nobody clicks the CTA (so you barely
   pay). Caveat: interaction quality is thin (one slide flip), so run a **4-6 stage document
   funnel** so people actually absorb who you are before Stage 3.

Companion metric: **RMR = Retargetable Member Rate** = % of impressions that become a
retargetable member (video = 50% view rate; static/document = engagement rate). It measures
how effective the creative is at getting the action you want.

See `05-benchmarks-optimization.md` for exactly how to build the retargeting audiences and
calculate CPRM/RMR in Campaign Manager.

## Budget-constrained variant

With limited budget, **consolidate**. One well-funded campaign beats twenty starving ad
sets. Fewer, more focused ad sets with concentrated spend, then let data tell you what to
scale.
