# Carnegie's How to Win Friends and Influence People - Quick Reference

## Part 1: Fundamental Techniques in Handling People

### 1. Don't Criticize, Condemn, or Complain
- Criticism puts people on the defensive immediately
- People never criticize themselves. They rationalize.
- People are creatures of emotion, not logic
- Short-term satisfaction of criticism is never worth the long-term resentment
- "A great man shows his greatness by the way he treats little men." (Carlyle)

**Marketing Application:** Never make prospects feel stupid for their current approach. Frame problems as external forces, not their failures. "The landscape has changed" > "You're behind."

### 2. Give Honest and Sincere Appreciation
- People crave a feeling of importance more than almost anything else
- Find what people want most and show them how to get it
- The deepest urge in human nature is the desire to be important
- Appreciation must be sincere. Flattery is counterfeit and fails.

**Marketing Application:** Validate the prospect's expertise before positioning your solution. "You already understand the challenge..." makes them feel respected and opens them to your message.

### 3. Arouse in the Other Person an Eager Want
- The only way to influence people is to talk about what THEY want
- "If there is any one secret of success, it lies in the ability to get the other person's point of view and see things from that person's angle" (Henry Ford)
- Frame everything in terms of their interests, not yours

**Marketing Application:** Every headline, every CTA should answer "What's in it for me?" from the prospect's perspective. Features mean nothing; outcomes mean everything.

## Part 2: Six Ways to Make People Like You

### 1. Become Genuinely Interested in Other People
- You make more friends in 2 months by being interested in them than in 2 years trying to get them interested in you
- Interest must be genuine or it backfires

**Marketing Application:** Research your audience deeply. Reference their specific challenges, industry dynamics, and aspirations. Generic messaging signals disinterest.

### 2. Smile
- Actions speak louder than words
- A smile communicates "I like you. I am glad to see you."
- Works even when unseen (over the phone, in writing)

**Marketing Application:** Tone matters. Warm, approachable copy > cold, corporate language. Enthusiasm is contagious even in text.

### 3. Remember and Use Names
- A person's name is the sweetest sound in any language
- Using someone's name shows respect and attention

**Marketing Application:** Personalization at scale. Use names in emails, reference their company, acknowledge their role. Generic "Dear Decision Maker" = delete.

### 4. Be a Good Listener: Let Others Talk
- "Exclusive attention to the person who is speaking to you is very important. Nothing else is so flattering as that." (Charles W. Eliot)
- The ability to listen is rarer than any other good trait

**Marketing Application:** Use surveys, reviews, call recordings to listen to your audience. Mirror their exact language back. Content that feels like "they read my mind" = trust.

### 5. Talk in Terms of the Other Person's Interests
- Anticipate what matters most to your audience
- Come prepared with knowledge of their world

**Marketing Application:** Lead with their pain points and goals, not your features. "You" should outnumber "We" by 3:1 minimum.

### 6. Make Others Feel Important, Sincerely
- People will do anything for someone who makes them feel truly important
- Small courtesies make indelible impressions

**Marketing Application:** "Leaders like you..." / "For teams serious about..." give them a reputation to live up to (see Part 4.7).

## Part 3: How to Win People to Your Way of Thinking

### 1. Avoid Arguments
- You can't win an argument. Even if you "win," you lose the other person.
- Show respect for other people's opinions

### 2. Never Say "You're Wrong"
- Strips people of self-dignity
- "I judge people by their own principles" (MLK)

### 3. If You're Wrong, Admit It Quickly
- Admitting mistakes takes all the fight out of the other person
- Creates goodwill and disarms criticism

### 4. Begin in a Friendly Way
- "A drop of honey catches more flies than a gallon of gall"
- Start warm, even when delivering hard truths

### 5. Get the Other Person Saying "Yes" (The Socratic Method)
- Start with questions they'll agree with
- Build momentum of agreement before the ask
- A chain of "yes" makes the final "yes" almost automatic

### 6. Let the Other Person Do Most of the Talking
- People prefer their own ideas
- Ask questions, then listen

### 7. Let Them Think It Was Their Idea
- "In every work of genius, we recognize our own rejected thoughts" (Emerson)
- Guide, don't prescribe

### 8. See Things from Their Perspective
- "How would I react if I were in their shoes?"
- Success depends on a sympathetic grasp of the other person's viewpoint

### 9. Appeal to Nobler Motives
- People want to feel they're acting from high-minded reasons
- Frame the decision in terms of values, not just utility

### 10. Dramatize Your Ideas
- Show, don't just tell
- Vivid demonstrations > dry explanations

### 11. Throw Down a Challenge
- People respond to competition and the desire to excel
- "Can you beat last quarter's results?"

## Part 4: Leadership Principles (for CTAs and Conversion)

### 1. Begin with Praise Before Criticism
- Sugar before medicine

### 2. Use "And" Instead of "But"
- "We're proud of your progress AND with continued effort..."
- "But" negates everything before it

### 3. Talk About Your Own Mistakes First
- Shared vulnerability builds trust

### 4. Ask Questions Instead of Giving Orders
- "What do you think about...?" > "You should..."

### 5. Let the Other Person Save Face
- Never back someone into a corner

### 6. Praise Every Improvement
- Even the slightest progress deserves recognition

### 7. Give a Reputation to Live Up To
- "If you give a person a fine reputation to live up to, they have a funny habit of actually living up to it"
- "Assume a virtue, if you have it not" (Shakespeare)

### 8. Make Faults Seem Easy to Correct
- Use encouragement liberally
- "You have an undeveloped flair for it"

### 9. Make People Happy About Doing What You Want
- Match your request to their actual benefits
- Couch requests in terms that vividly portray the benefits THEY receive
